### What we learned, and what changes

1. **The first two attempts were refused by my own script, and rightly so.** (1) It expected an `Env`
   tag on the machine; the auto-scaling group passes only `Name` and `Project`, so the guard could never
   pass on staging. (2) Its "healthy to begin with" check used the gateway's `/healthz`, which is 503 on
   staging for ever because it also checks a language model staging does not have (the staging smoke
   test already knew this). Both were fixed (the group name is the identity; healthy now means the
   gateway answers and its database check is ok) and neither ended anything. The guard doing its job
   was a refusal, not a mistake: it failed shut. Tests with a fake cloud could not have found either.
2. **The script's step times were checked against the cluster's clocks** because the first version
   stamped each step at the start of the check that saw it, and a check on a booting machine blocks for
   up to 90 s. The two agreed to within about 20 s. The script now stamps after the check returns.
3. **Recovery is faster than assumed** (about 2 minutes, not the 5 to 10 the plan allowed). One run,
   so the plan's allowance stays as the target; the figure to expect is "a few minutes", and the
   bootstrap on a fresh machine is not the slow part it was feared to be.
4. **An outside "still alive" check is justified, with a two-minute-plus design target.** Planned in
   `KAV-83`; this run is its evidence.
5. **Not tested here:** that an approval waiting for a person survives the loss. By design it does
   (approvals are rows on the database server, one decision per action and one execution per action are
   unique in the schema) but it was not run. A spot-reclaim's two-minute warning was also not simulated.
   Both are noted for `KAV-83`.
