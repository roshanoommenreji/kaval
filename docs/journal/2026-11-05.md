## Three leftover backups deleted; a flaw found on the way (`KAV-127`, `KAV-128`)

- With Roshan's "go", the three 10 GB snapshots the nightly stop had left behind (22 Oct, 27 Oct, 4 Nov) were deleted. A guard re-read each one first and would have deleted nothing if any no longer said `Env=staging`, `kaval-hard-stop-pre-stop`, 10 GB; a dry run passed on the real ones the night before, and the same guard refused one of production's. Afterwards the account held exactly the 20 snapshots of production's database, untouched. At most about $1.50 a month saved.
- **Found while looking at the list before deleting:** on 2026-11-04 at 03:15 UTC staging's snapshot schedule took a snapshot of **production's** data disk. AWS's Data Lifecycle Manager covers every disk that has *any one* of a schedule's target tags, not all of them, and both environments' disks carry `Role=database-data`; the module's "Name as well as Role" never separated them. CloudTrail shows that schedule was created at the 3 Nov staging build and deleted by the 4 Nov teardown. The harm is one redundant 20 GB (incremental) snapshot that nothing will expire. [ADR-0008](../adr/0008-production-database-on-its-own-server.md) amended; fix filed as `KAV-128` (target the `Name` tag alone, with a test; production's plan will show one in-place change, applied on Roshan's go).
