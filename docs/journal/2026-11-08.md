## The overnight proof worked: each schedule copied only its own disk, and the teardown left nothing (`KAV-127`, `KAV-128`)

Roshan said `go overnight proof` at 22:58 IST on 7 Nov and `go morning teardown` at 16:37 on 8 Nov. Between the two, nothing touched staging. The full numbers are in [Lab 78](../labs/lab-78-backup-copies-that-belong-to-the-right-environment.md) Part 4a; this is the story of it.

**The build (22:57 to 23:04).** The start script's guards passed: on `main`, clean, in sync, containing #322, nothing of staging in the account, and inside the 22:45 to 01:15 window. The saved plan read exactly `Plan: 93 to add, 0 to change, 0 to destroy.`, with no mention of production, and the apply read `93 added, 0 changed, 0 destroyed`. `main` now pins staging at `sha-e7c3cd2`, a build staging had not run before, so whether the apps would come up was the one open worry (the proof is about the disk's copies, not the apps). All nine came up `Running 1/1` about two minutes after the apply and the start-up report said `ok`. A read-only check at 23:04: two schedules, each aimed at its own disk's `Name` tag alone; copies by label, staging 0 and production 21.

**The morning, read-only (16:32; the plan said any time after 09:00).** Staging held exactly two copies, as predicted once production's schedule was fixed (three before it): the nightly stop's, made at 02:00 IST, and one from staging's own schedule at 08:59 IST. Production's disk gained exactly one copy since the build, from production's own schedule at 08:53 IST, and none from staging's. By schedule id, production's schedule owned 8 copies, all labelled `Env=prod`, and staging's owned 1, labelled staging. Both runs came 23 to 29 minutes after 03:00 UTC; a schedule does not run at the exact minute.

**One production copy was missing from the list recorded before the build, and I checked why before saying anything.** The count was still 21, because production's schedule made one and expired its oldest. CloudTrail showed a single `DeleteSnapshot` since the build, at 09:39 IST, by `DataLifecycleManager` (AWS's own schedule service), and the teardown had not run yet. So it was production's schedule keeping its seven, not damage. Without that check the "copies missing now" line of the morning script could have been misread as the proof failing.

**The teardown (16:37 to 16:40).** `make staging-down`, through the proof's wrapper: `Apply complete! Resources: 0 added, 0 changed, 92 destroyed.`, then the detached data disk, then `Deleting snapshot ...` for both of staging's copies, and `Left tagged Env=staging: 0 instances, 0 volumes, 0 VPCs, 0 snapshots`. The old script would have left the nightly stop's copy. A read-only sweep of the whole account afterwards found nothing of staging (no server, disk, copy, schedule, network, address, bucket, function, log group, parameter, role or rule), production's count still 21 and the same single missing copy.

**How the go words were handled.** A bare `continue`, and a `yes` that followed an interrupted check, were not read as the teardown go: the morning check ran (it only looks), I said so, and the teardown ran only after the exact phrase `go morning teardown`.

**A small finding, not changed.** Production's schedule owns one older copy, from 6 Oct, of an earlier production data disk (a different disk id). It keeps seven copies of each disk, counted per disk, so a copy of a disk that no longer exists is never expired. Cents a month; it is production's and deleting it needs Roshan's go, so it stays (recorded in ADR-0078, "Noticed, not changed").

**Cost.** About 15 to 20 cents (my estimate, not yet on the bill): three hours running at about $0.045 an hour, then a stopped staging with its disk and two copies for about 14 hours.
