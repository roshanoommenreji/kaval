## The v0.4.4 release passed staging (`KAV-162`, `KAV-150`)

On his typed `go staging smoke` (17 Nov) after the release round: `release-prepare` opened #449, he merged it, `release.yml` built the images and tagged **v0.4.4** (`sha-f280013`), and the bot's staging pin #450 was merged next. v0.4.4 carries the gateway without its unused Kubernetes login (`KAV-162`) and the corrected wording in the service code (`KAV-150`).

Terraform gave up and left the group marked as failed. Cost while it waited: the database server only, about 2 to 3 cents an hour. He was asked (wait or delete) and answered "continue" the next morning.
- **AWS did free the capacity** (the group launched a server at about 10:00 IST on 17 Nov), but the nightly 02:00 stop had already parked staging by the time we came back.
- **The second build, 18 Nov 08:15 to 08:32.** The plan was shown first: 7 to add, 1 to change (the server's base image moved to AWS's newer one), 1 to replace (the failed group), all inside staging, nothing of production. Applied as planned: 7 added, 1 changed, 1 destroyed. The resumed staging got a fresh cluster; all 9 apps ran.
- **Smoke test 7 of 7 at 08:52 IST** on `sha-f280013`: pods ready and no restarts, every service on the tag, running digests equal to the registry's, the database migrated to `d4f7a2c9e815`, both API calls answered 200, and the build is a tagged release (production runs v0.4.3). - **Teardown at 08:56:** 93 destroyed, plus the data disk and 2 backup copies; the sweep was clean (nothing of staging left); production's 26 backup copies unchanged. About 10 cents in all, most of it the stalled night.
- **Worth keeping:** a stalled capacity shortage is not a failure of the build, so the right answer is to wait, not to rebuild or change the instance type; a failed first apply leaves the group "tainted" and the next apply replaces it, which is expected; and a staging left half-built overnight is parked by the 02:00 stop, so finishing it means a resume.

Left for his own gos: `go promote v0.4.4`, then the check at production's next start-up; only then `KAV-162` and `KAV-150` are ticked and moved to Done.

On his `go production start-up` and `apply production` (09:15 and 09:18 IST): the plan was shown first (0 to add, 2 to change, 0 to destroy: the group from 0 to 1, and the server's base image moved to AWS's newer one), preflight passed (all four images `sha-f280013` in the registry), v0.4.4 has no database migration against v0.4.3. The database came up and passed its health check at 09:18; then AWS answered "insufficient t4g.medium capacity" in all three Mumbai zones, every retry, until at least 10:10. Terraform gave up; the group stayed at 1 and kept retrying. He chose to keep waiting (about 2 cents an hour) and to build the fix.

- **The fix, `KAV-163`:** the node module takes a list of sizes (`t4g.medium`, then `c6g.large`: the same 2 CPUs and 4 GB, arm64, a different family so a different pool; $0.0224 vs $0.0426 an hour from AWS's price list) and the group is a prioritised, On-Demand mixed-instances policy over it. Spot's setting moved from the launch template to the group's distribution. 15 offline tests; `terraform validate` passes for staging and production.
- **Ruled out:** another region (a migration, not a fix), `m6g.medium` (one CPU), `t4g.large` (the same family, twice the price), a capacity reservation (pays all day), waiting every time.
- **Left:** a staging session with `c6g.large` first (own go), production's plan and apply (own go), then production's check at its start-up; the pending v0.4.4 promotion check rides on the same start-up.
